A few years ago I asked myself why I got good at hockey. Talent, a bit. My parents, more: they backed something I loved before I could hold a stick properly. But mostly coaches. How I work

Most companies don't lack ideas.

What they lack is focus. They start too many things and finish too few: a transformation programme, a performance programme, a cost initiative. Each makes sense on its own. Together they are never one system.

So I build the system first: one goal, clear priorities, simple standards for how the work gets done, and a steady rhythm of review. That's what makes change last. Only then can you see what really moves results, and what can go. That's why I start with the same three questions. What are we trying to achieve? What has to happen to get there? And what can we stop doing?

Four parts make it work:

  • Direction & value

    One clear goal, broken down so every team knows what it contributes. Then sort the work by value: what creates it gets our energy, what only supports it gets leaner, and what does neither stops.

  • Execution & learning

    Write the assumption down. Decide when you're 70 percent sure. Act, measure, adjust, and learn from what happened. Then do it again. That's continuous improvement, the Lean way I was trained in, run at the speed of a start-up.

  • Data & AI

    Measure what matters, and use data to take opinion out of decisions. Use AI to take routine work off people's desks, so their time goes where judgement matters. I build my own agents and automations, so I know what works and what doesn't.

  • Leadership & people

    The hard part hasn't changed. People still have to want to get better, and leaders have to make that possible, starting with themselves. Every day is day one.
